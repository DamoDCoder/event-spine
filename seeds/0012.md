**A reader using a segment compaction had closed.** No fault injected: a cursor
reads part of a segment, a compaction replaces that segment, and the next read
gets `file is closed`.

Compaction writes the survivors to a temporary file, renames it into place, and
closes the handle it replaced. A live `Reader` is holding that handle, and worse
than the handle being closed, its byte position points into a layout that no
longer exists — the replacement file has the same offsets in different places.

Both are use-after-compaction, and neither was reachable by any seed for four
milestones because every workload read one record at a time through `Log.Read`
and let go of the cursor in between. The restart workload keeps one alive across
compactions on purpose, and found this on the first sweep that included it, in
sixteen of two hundred seeds.

`docs/log-design.md` predicted a reader problem here and named the wrong one:

> Readers must tolerate gaps, and the simulation harness must inject them,
> because "reader assumes offsets are contiguous after compaction" is the exact
> bug this design invites.

Gaps were handled in M2 and tested from both directions. The bug that was
actually there is that the reader's *handle and position* do not survive the
segment being replaced, which is a different sentence about the same interaction.
