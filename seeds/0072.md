**A commit that returned an error still took effect.** One injected fsync
failure, no crash and no corruption: a group resumed at offset 16 after its
`Commit(16)` had failed, when the last commit that returned was 4.

`Group.Commit` writes the record and then syncs it. When the sync fails the
record is already on the disk, and a restart short of a power cut reads it back
— the page cache holds what the failed sync did not flush. Replay then takes it
as the group's position.

The log cannot promise otherwise without writing a second record to confirm the
first, which would double the cost of every commit to close a window that
at-least-once delivery already covers. So this was fixed in the contract rather
than the code, and the invariant was made precise instead of being made
comfortable: **a group never resumes at an offset nobody asked it to.** A failed
commit can only move a group to a position the consumer itself named, and
therefore to one it had already processed.

The seed is kept because that invariant is easy to strengthen back into
something false. A checker asserting "the recovered position is the last commit
that returned" passes today and fails the moment an fsync does.
