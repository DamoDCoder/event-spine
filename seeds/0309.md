**The checker again, and the most interesting one of those so far.** It reported
the log returning the wrong record:

```
durable record 6 came back as {Key:acct-06 Time:21 ...}, want {Key:acct-06 Time:13 ...}
```

The log was right. Corruption truncated it, recovery took the tail back below
offset 6, and a later append was assigned offset 6 for a **different record**.
The witness was still holding the record that used to be there.

That is a real property worth being explicit about: **offsets are stable while
their records survive, and not across data loss.** A truncation moves the tail
back, and the offsets above it are handed out again. A consumer holding an
offset from before the loss is pointing at something else — which is what a
leader epoch is for in systems that solve it, and the spine does not.

The witness now tracks the lowest tail the log ever regressed to and stops
vouching for anything above it. Everything below is still checked exactly:
corruption never excuses a wrong record where the checker still knows what the
right one was.

Four failures in one sweep were this same mistake wearing different clothes — a
commit past a truncated tail, a snapshot past one, a reader restarted from the
beginning, and this. All four were the witness's model of the log going stale
while the log itself behaved correctly.
